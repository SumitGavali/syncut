How it Works

From raw footage to rough cut.

01

Choose a reference file

Pick your cleanest audio source — usually a boom or lav mic. Every camera gets aligned to it.

02

Add your camera sources

Drop in every angle you shot. SyncCut extracts audio from each and runs cross-correlation against the reference.

03

Review offset & confidence

See the exact offset, drift rate, and a calibrated confidence badge for every camera before anything gets trimmed or cut.

04

Export & edit

Pull a synced multi-track timeline, or let SyncCut suggest camera switches and export a rough cut — ready to open in your NLE.

Why this exists
SC

I built SyncCut because I was tired of wasting the first hour of every edit syncing footage by hand.

I edit multi-cam podcasts, interviews, and music sessions. Every single project started the same way: drag waveforms, guess offsets, wonder why camera C drifted by 40 milliseconds halfway through the take. It's boring, it's error-prone, and it has nothing to do with the actual craft of editing.

So I built the tool I wanted. It uses audio cross-correlation to find the true offset between sources, scores its own confidence so you know when to trust it, and exports a real FCPXML your NLE actually opens — not a text file shaped like an EDL.

It works. I use it on my own projects. What I don't have yet is 20 other editors stress-testing it on footage that isn't mine. That's what the beta is for — I want to find the edge cases I haven't hit yet, with editors who'll tell me when it's wrong.
